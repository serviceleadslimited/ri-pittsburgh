## How to compare a roof-inspection visit

Start with the problem, not the company name. A ceiling stain after rain, storm debris, a roof near the end of its useful life, a home purchase, or a contractor’s replacement recommendation can require different inspection scope. A free contractor visit can be the practical first step for a repair or condition concern. A paid independent assessment may make more sense when the value is a neutral written opinion for a sale, purchase, dispute, HOA matter, or formal documentation.

When you call, ask what will actually be checked. A useful answer should cover the roof surface, flashing, valleys, penetrations, gutters and drainage, and accessible attic or interior evidence when it relates to the concern. Ask which parts cannot be accessed safely and how that limitation changes the conclusion. A company should be able to explain the difference between observed damage, ordinary wear, and a condition that needs more evaluation.

Then ask what you will receive. Photographs and a written scope can make it easier to compare recommendations later, especially after a storm or when more than one contractor is involved. A written estimate is not automatically the same thing as a formal independent inspection report. Tell the company up front if another party—such as a buyer, seller, insurer, or HOA—requires a particular type of documentation.

Finally, compare the recommendation, not only the price. Ask whether a targeted repair is reasonable, what evidence supports replacement if it is proposed, and whether temporary protection is needed while you decide. Do not let urgency replace an explanation. If the recommendation does not match what was observed or you are unsure about a full replacement, get another opinion before signing.

## Questions to ask before booking

1. Is the inspection free, and what will it include?
2. Will the roof be accessed when safe, and which areas may be limited by height, pitch, weather, or material condition?
3. Will you inspect accessible attic or interior evidence when a leak or stain is involved?
4. Will I receive photographs, written findings, a repair estimate, or a formal report?
5. Can you explain repair alternatives before recommending replacement?
6. Is there any obligation to hire you after the inspection?
7. What insurance and applicable Pennsylvania registration information should I verify before authorizing work?
8. If this involves a claim, what can you document without promising coverage or approval?
